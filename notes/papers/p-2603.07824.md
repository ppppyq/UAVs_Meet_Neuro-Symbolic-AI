# Reasoning Knowledge-Gap in Drone Planning via LLM-based Active Elicitation

Reviewed 2026-09-22 · fulltext_reviewed · included · human review pending.

This review supersedes the 2026-09-20 note; earlier retrieval and screening history remains in `data/papers.json`.

## Neural–symbolic interface

A vision-language model processes RGB-D observations and constructs a local semantic map; an LLM selects optimal binary questions for active human elicitation.

The Minimal Information Neuro-Symbolic Tree (MINT) explicitly structures obstacle and goal knowledge gaps; a hierarchical planner evaluates trajectory cost/divergence and goal entropy over hypothesis branches.

The VLM identifies semantic uncertainty; MINT represents it as a symbolic tree; the LLM chooses a binary query; the human answer prunes the tree and updates the semantic map and plan.

## Evidence and limitations

MINT structures knowledge gaps about obstacles and goals into a queryable neuro-symbolic tree, uses an LLM to ask optimal binary questions, and updates plans after human responses. It integrates VLM perception, voice interaction, and low-level UAV control in NVIDIA Isaac simulations and real-world quadrotor trials.

Simulation success/query counts and a separate 20-trial physical demonstration are small, task-specific results. Human answers are treated as reliable; model details, query optimality and failure coverage need further validation. This is an AAAI Spring Symposium paper, not an AAAI main-conference paper.

## Classification

Primary theme is collaboration and interaction because active human-UAV knowledge elicitation is the central contribution; MINT reasoning also supports mission planning as a secondary theme.

Meets the four scope criteria after source-based review of the neural component, explicit symbolic mechanism, functional coupling and described UAV validation. Inclusion is a review decision, not human review or independent replication.
