## 1. Neural part

The system uses an INT8-quantized SegFormer-B0 with geometric post-processing.
It consumes onboard visual input and constructs a probabilistic semantic scene
graph (PSSG) representing terrain and obstacle-relevant semantics.

## 2. Symbolic part

Deterministic Scallop rules express terrain-flatness, obstacle-clearance, and
spatial-consistency constraints over the PSSG. Rules are authored offline with
LLM assistance and refined with a human in the loop. The runtime does not query
the LLM.

## 3. Coupling

Neural perception populates the PSSG; symbolic rules then evaluate candidate
landing regions over that explicit representation. The documented direction is
neural-to-symbolic, with symbolic evaluation occurring at runtime.

## 4. UAV validation

The full text reports 72 simulated scenarios with 61 successful assessments,
and 100 hardware-in-the-loop profiling trials. No real-flight validation was
identified in the reviewed version. The record therefore uses `simulation` and
`hardware_in_loop`, not `real_flight`.

## 5. Limitations

Reported evidence does not include real flight. Venue metadata is unresolved:
the arXiv comments say "Accepted to the IROS 2026", while the journal reference
says "Proceedings of the ACM on Software Engineering, Vol. 3, FSE, Article
FSE146, July 2026", with related DOI `10.1145/3808153` that has not been
confirmed against a publisher record. Rule authoring relies on an offline
LLM-assisted and human-in-the-loop workflow even though runtime execution is
deterministic.
